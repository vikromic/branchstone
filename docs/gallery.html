Of Ash and Flowers

2025

Born Of Burn

Materials

Ponderosa pine, burned wood pieces, acrylic on canvas

Dimensions
16 x 20 in
Year
2025

Story

After a fire, what remains is never the same as what went in. When the heat fades and everything goes quiet, something slowly begins to return.

These flowers don’t belong to gardens. They come from burned ground, from places shaped by loss and endurance at the same time. Ash becomes their soil. Leftover heat traces their beginnings.

They carry the memory of what happened here, and they remind me that beauty can exist even after damage.

Story

Some things really do bloom straight through the burn. This piece is about what quietly comes back after an ending — when something has been stripped down, scorched, undone, and still life finds a way to return.

The textures come from real petals and plants I collected, dried, and preserved. I placed them the way memories tend to settle — not perfectly, but intentionally.

Of Ash and Flowers

2025

Spring Fire

Materials

Red wood root piece, burned wood pigment, burned wood pieces, acrylic on canvas.

Dimensions
34 x 24 in
Year
2025

Story

It begins with a whisper. Nature waking slowly, brushing off the last frost, dreaming of dandelions and light. Tiny first messengers move through the hush: a beetle, a melt, a yellow bloom.

And then the world splits open. A blaze, wild and vengeful, turning air to ash, green to black. Even the sky forgets how to hold still.

For a while, silence feels like loss. But beneath the burn, roots remember.

Life is patient. What seems lost is only waiting. And when it returns, it rises hungry, wild, and full of fire-fed strength.

This work holds part of that story - crafted around a redwood root from a centuries-old tree that did not survive the flames, but still speaks through form, colour and texture.

Of Ash and Flowers

2024

Fragments of Light

Materials

Sago palm seed coating, algae, sand, acrylic on canvas panel

Dimensions
20 x 16 in
Year
2024

Story

This work is dedicated to the loss of Iryna Tsybukh — a figure whose courage, honesty, and love of life became a symbol for many in Ukraine.

Though she was taken by the war, the values she lived by cannot be erased. As Iryna wrote, “To have the strength to be free, one must be brave.”

The piece reflects the emotional weight of losing something monumental — something that should not have disappeared. Fragments of Iryna’s words appear in the background, carrying the strength and inspiration she shared with others.

The colors speak of hope and freedom — of what endures, and of what continues to live in our hearts. The textures reflect how deeply grief can tear through the soul.

32

Magnet

2026

Magnet

Materials

Printed dragons, river sand, coil crumbs, bohemian truffle, acrylic on canvas.

Dimensions
24 x 18 in
Year
2026

Story

Magnet reflects the idea that opposites are not rivals, but parts of the same structure. They often share one origin, one field, one logic. Their difference is what keeps the system alive, creates tension.

Like a good old concert of yin and yang, these forces are defined by contrast, but neither exists on its own. They are in constant relation, drawn toward each other, shaped by distance, tension, and orientation. The movement between them never stops, even when they do not touch.

This work is about harmony as interaction, the balance between resistance and drawing. About how opposing qualities search for one another, respond, adjust, and remain connected through an invisible pull.
